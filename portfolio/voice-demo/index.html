Voice Agent Design — Finding the Brand's Tessitura

Fictional brand · Mother’s Day Gift Rescue · Recorded design study

Core thesis: In vocal performance, tessitura is the range where a voice sounds most natural — narrower than the full range. Every brand has a tessitura too. This case study finds ACME's: a 5-state pacing model where each conversational state sits at a measured point within the brand's emotional range (0.83–1.30 speedAlpha). The improvement comes from constraining to tessitura, not from a different voice.

Design question: pacing by conversational state

This study varies pacing by conversational state: acknowledgment, options, boundary, confirmation and close. A global speed setting does not describe all of those choices. Listen for the pauses and the separation of critical details. Other voices and controls need their own evaluation.

What This Comparison Shows

The displayed measurements describe these recordings; they do not measure customer trust or business outcomes. The boundary state creates a distinct register for honesty moments. Critical details are isolated instead of buried in one dense confirmation sentence. Whether finer controls improve consistency needs a separate test. The comparison holds the voice constant and changes the pacing design.
